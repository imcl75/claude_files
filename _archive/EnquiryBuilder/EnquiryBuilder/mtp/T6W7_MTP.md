# T6W7 States of Matter — Medium Term Plan (CONFIRMED, in use)

**Confirmed by Innes:** 2026-07-11, chose this over the alternative Claude-drafted plan (`T6W7_draft_A_NOT_IN_USE.md`, kept for reference only, not used).

**Year group:** Y4 Chemistry
**Week:** Monday 13 – Friday 17 July 2026
**Key question:** Can materials change their state?
**Outcome type:** No challenge/investigation outcome — knowledge and skills focus

**Cross-curricular links:** water cycle (Geography, L3); line graphs (Maths, L4)
**Misconceptions addressed:** steam is invisible (L2); melting ≠ dissolving (L5)
**Total images needed:** 19 (13 Higgsfield, 4 dall-e)

---

## L1 — Monday 13 July: What are the three states of matter?

**LO:** compare and group materials as solids, liquids or gases using their observable properties

- Recall: activates Y2 atoms/water knowledge
- We Do: image grid (2×4) — sort 8 materials (ice, water, steam, wood, sand, milk, balloon, honey)
- I Do: annotated particle model diagram (solid/liquid/gas arrangements)
- Provocation: oobleck — "Is this a solid or a liquid?"
- You Do: sort 12 material cards with reasons

**Images:** 8-photo grid (Higgsfield, white background) + particle model diagram (dall-e)

---

## L2 — Tuesday 14 July: What happens when materials are heated?

**LO:** describe and explain what happens when materials are heated, using melting, boiling and evaporating

- Recall: builds on L1 particle arrangements
- I Do: comparison — solid wax vs melted wax
- I Do: heating particle diagram (solid → liquid → gas with temperatures)
- Provocation: vigorously boiling water
- Misconception slide: is the white cloud above a kettle steam?
- You Do: annotate the heating diagram on the LP, add 0°C and 100°C

**Images:** wax comparison ×2 (Higgsfield) + heating diagram (dall-e) + boiling water provocation (Higgsfield)

---

## L3 — Wednesday 15 July: What happens when materials are cooled?

**LO:** describe what happens when materials are cooled and explain that changing state is reversible

- Recall: builds on L2
- Provocation opens the lesson: "Where has this water come from?" (condensation on a cold glass)
- I Do: full two-way reversible changes diagram
- I Do: water cycle cross-curricular slide (CLF link: States of Matter → Geography water cycle)
- You Do: label all four arrows on a blank diagram with melting/freezing/evaporating/condensing + temperatures

**Images:** condensation provocation (Higgsfield) + reversible changes diagram (dall-e) + water cycle diagram (dall-e)

---

## L4 — Thursday 16 July: How do scientists measure changing state?

**LO:** use a thermometer to take accurate temperature readings and collect, record and present data

- Recall: checks vocabulary and temperatures from L2/L3
- I Do: thermometer reading diagram (how to read accurately, eye level, meniscus)
- We Do: walkthrough of the ice-melting investigation method
- You Do: practical — record temperature every 2 minutes, results table, line graph, written conclusion

**Images:** thermometer diagram (dall-e)

---

## L5 — Friday 17 July: Can ALL materials change their state?

**LO:** explain that every material can change state and that different materials have different melting and boiling points

- Recall: reviews the week
- I Do: image grid (2×3) — six materials with melting points (chocolate 34°C, butter 32°C, wax 64°C, aluminium 660°C, iron 1538°C, nitrogen -196°C)
- Provocation: lava — "Can rock change its state?"
- Misconception slide: melting vs dissolving
- You Do: written answer to the key question using 3+ examples

**Images:** 6-material grid (Higgsfield, white background) + lava provocation (Higgsfield)
