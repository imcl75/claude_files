# Enquiry Planner

## Purpose

Produces a detailed planning DOCX for a Year 4 CLF subject enquiry unit at
Wallscourt Farm Academy. Subjects covered: history, geography, science,
computing, citizenship, DT, art, music, languages.

The DOCX contains:
1. **Unit Overview Table** — metadata, key question, both outcomes, phase
   structure, subject skills, differentiation overview.
2. **Lesson Plans** — one section per lesson with LO table, overview paragraph,
   and I Do / We Do / You Do breakdown.

## Step 1: Gather Inputs via Dialogue

Do NOT assume any inputs. Ask Innes for the following all at once:

```
To plan your enquiry I need a few details:

1. Subject — history, geography, science, computing, citizenship, DT, art,
   music, or languages?
2. Topic — what is the enquiry about? (e.g. Anglo-Saxons, South America,
   Electricity)
3. Key question — the enquiry question framing the unit (I can suggest one)
4. Length — 1 week (~7 lessons), 2 weeks (~14 lessons), or 3 weeks (~21 lessons)?
5. Writing outcome genre — inform, explain, or argue? (I'll warn if overused)
6. Enquiry outcome — what will pupils produce to showcase their learning?
   (exhibition, display, presentation, model, fact file, etc. — I can suggest)
7. Any specific lesson content or focus points you want included?
8. NC links to flag — or shall I infer these?
```

Once inputs are gathered, **propose the Key Question and suggest an Enquiry
Outcome** if not provided, then confirm with Innes before proceeding.

**Writing genre warning:** If Innes selects "explain" twice in a row or "argue"
at all in Year 4, flag this and ask if it's intentional. "Inform" is the most
common and appropriate genre at this stage.

## Step 2: Plan the Phase Structure

### Phase Philosophy

Enquiry phases are **flexible** — do not use fixed labels like Immerse/Have-a-Go/
Challenge. Instead, infer sensible phases from subject and content. The three
typical phases are:

| Phase | Core Purpose | Typical Labels |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Phase 1 | Build knowledge and vocabulary; introduce subject skills and concepts | "Explore", "Discover", "Know", "Locate" |
| Phase 2 | Apply knowledge; practise enquiry skills and writing skills in focused tasks | "Investigate", "Apply", "Compare", "Analyse" |
| Phase 3 | Create both outcomes; edit, present, and reflect | "Create", "Communicate", "Showcase", "Produce" |

Choose labels that match the subject flavour. Examples:
- History: Discover → Investigate → Communicate
- Geography: Locate and Observe → Compare and Analyse → Create and Present
- Science: Question and Predict → Test and Observe → Conclude and Report

### Phase Length Splits

| Total Lessons | Phase 1 | Phase 2 | Phase 3 |
|---------------|---------|---------|---------|
| 7 (1 week) | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| 14 (2 weeks) | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| 21 (3 weeks) | 8 | 6 | 7 |

### Phase 3: The Two Outcomes

Phase 3 must deliver **both outcomes**:

**Writing outcome** (inform/explain/argue):
- 1 lesson: model text analysis / shared write stimulus
- 1-2 lessons: drafting (shared → guided → independent)
- 1 lesson: editing and publishing (no "best copy" lesson — edit IS the lesson)

**Enquiry outcome** (exhibition, display, presentation, model, etc.):
- 1 lesson: planning and designing the outcome
- 1-2 lessons: creating it
- Final lesson: sharing, presenting, and reflecting — **no separate publishing**

In a 7-lesson unit, outcomes are tightly interleaved. In a 14-21 lesson unit,
they can sit in parallel strands within Phase 3.

### Subject-Specific Phase Content Guidance

**History:** Phase 1 = timeline, key people, events, vocabulary; Phase 2 = source
analysis, interpretations, cause and consequence; Phase 3 = writing (usually an
information text about the civilisation/period) + enquiry outcome (e.g. museum
display, annotated timeline, role-play exhibition).

**Geography:** Phase 1 = locating on maps, physical/human features, vocabulary;
Phase 2 = comparison fieldwork/research, maps, data; Phase 3 = writing (usually
an information text comparing two places) + enquiry outcome (e.g. travel guide,
annotated map, fact file presentation).

**Science:** Phase 1 = key concepts, vocabulary, predictions, prior knowledge;
Phase 2 = investigations, observations, recording results; Phase 3 = scientific
report (written outcome) + enquiry outcome (e.g. poster, presentation to class,
model, science fair display).

### HISTORY — Being a Historian

**Disciplinary skills** (used to frame LOs and lesson focus):
1. **Chronology** — sequencing events, timelines, understanding concurrent eras
2. **Sources and Evidence** — using artefacts, pictures, databases, written sources
3. **Questioning and Understanding** — asking historically valid questions, exploring
   causes and consequences
4. **Interpretations** — comparing accounts, understanding why accounts differ,
   describing beliefs/experiences of people

**Substantive concepts** (one per enquiry; choose the most relevant):
- **Civilisation** — "A civilisation is a group of people with their own languages
  and way of life. Civilised people live in organised groups like towns."
- **Invasion** — "To enter by force and gain control as an enemy."
- **Monarchy** — "A type of government where a single person is the lead. They are
  known as the Monarch."
- **Empire** — "A name for a group of countries ruled by a powerful leader who
  imposes its rule on people. Empires are a sign of power and wealth."
- **Revolution** — "A sudden, sharp change in something."

**Y4 CLF curriculum contexts:**
- Anglo-Saxons and Vikings (AD 410–1066): Invasion, Civilisation
- Mayan Civilisation (250 BC–AD 900): Civilisation (contrast with British history)
- Cross-curricular: geography of Mesoamerica within Maya enquiry is encouraged

**Y4 disciplinary expectations (Chronology):** Describe and place events in order;
use appropriate dates; label timelines with words and phrases.
**Y4 disciplinary expectations (Sources and Evidence):** Can suggest suitable
sources; use more than one source; describe different accounts and explain why
accounts may differ.
**Y4 disciplinary expectations (Questioning):** Use evidence to ask questions;
suggest causes and consequences.
**Y4 disciplinary expectations (Interpretations):** Give broad overview of life
in the period; compare with other areas of the world; describe characteristic
features including ideas, beliefs, attitudes and experiences of men, women and
children.

### GEOGRAPHY — Being a Geographer

**Disciplinary skills** (used to frame LOs and lesson focus):
1. **Map Skills** — maps, atlases, globes, Google Maps, 8-point compass, 6-figure
   grid references, OS maps, digital mapping
2. **Fieldwork** — observe, measure, record; sketch maps, plans, graphs, digital
   tech; questionnaires/surveys
3. **Observing** — books, maps, atlases, internet, satellite/aerial photos; human
   and physical features
4. **Questioning** — ask and answer questions; share ideas; high-quality
   geographical questions
5. **Concluding** — collect and record evidence; record in diagrams, charts, extended
   writing; make comparisons

**Substantive concepts** (one or more per enquiry):
- **Place / Space / Scale** — location, features, zoom-in/zoom-out approach; local,
  regional, global patterns
- **Physical and Human Processes** — how physical and human features form and change
- **Environmental Impact and Sustainability** — human impact on environments; capacity
  of environment to support life
- **Cultural Awareness and Diversity** — social/cultural interests; how people use,
  adapt, value environments and identities
- **Interconnection** — no geographical feature exists in isolation; links between
  features, places, events, people

**Y4 CLF curriculum contexts:**
- UK: London as capital city; SE England counties; significant cities of England
- Global: South America (Brazil, Ecuador, Chile, Bolivia, Colombia); latitude,
  longitude, hemispheres, tropics
- Contrasting study: England vs Peru/Brazil (biomes, climate zones, topography,
  land use, natural resources)

**Key Y4 geography vocabulary:** hemisphere, latitude, longitude, time zones,
equator, Tropic of Cancer, Tropic of Capricorn, biome, vegetation belt, climate
zone, topography, land use, natural resources, renewable/non-renewable

---

### SCIENCE — Being a Scientist

**Disciplinary skills** (used to frame LOs and lesson focus):
1. **Plan and Ask Questions** — ask relevant questions; select appropriate equipment;
   make reasoned predictions
2. **Test** — set up simple fair tests; control variables; sort evidence
3. **Observe and Measure** — careful observations; accurate measurements; use range
   of equipment including thermometers and data loggers
4. **Record and Present** — bar charts, tables, simple scientific report (plan,
   method, results, conclusion)
5. **Conclude** — draw conclusions; use scientific language; look for patterns;
   make predictions; look for patterns in results

**Y4 CLF science topics and key concepts:**
- **Electricity** (Physics): complete circuits, gaps, components, conductors/insulators
- **Sound** (Physics): vibrations, pitch, volume, ear/voice box
- **Classification of Animals** (Biology): vertebrates/invertebrates; fish,
  amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals; environmental change
- **Muscular and Skeletal System** (Biology): bones, muscles, tendons, ligaments,
  voluntary/involuntary; X-rays
- **Materials / States of Matter** (Physics/Chemistry): solids/liquids/gases;
  changing state; temperature; melting/boiling points

**Working scientifically at Y4 (Lower KS2):**
- Ask relevant questions with reasoned prediction
- Select appropriate equipment
- Set up simple fair tests; begin discussing variables
- Make careful observations; take accurate measurements
- Collect, record and present results using bar charts and tables
- Write simple scientific report with plan, method, results and conclusion
- Draw simple conclusions; use scientific language; look for patterns

## Step 4: Differentiation (A / Y / O / D)

All enquiries use the same four differentiation bands as writing:

| Band | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **A** | At an Earlier Stage — working 1+ year below Y4 expectations |
| **Y** | Yet to be on track — securing Y3, working into Y4 |
| **O** | On track — working within Y4 curriculum |
| **D** | Greater Depth — applying Y4 skills with confidence and independence |

For each **subject disciplinary skill** used in the enquiry, generate A/Y/O/D
descriptors showing how that skill looks at each level. Include these in the Unit
Overview Table as a differentiation grid. Example for History → Sources:

| Skill | A | Y | O | D |
|-------|---|---|---|---|
| Sources and Evidence | Sorts artefacts into then/now with support | Identifies one source and states what it tells us | Uses two or more sources; notes where accounts differ | Selects sources independently; explains why accounts might differ and what this tells us |

Generate these descriptors from the Y4 CLF expectations for the subject. Flag
to Innes that these are generated for his review and editing.

### Section 2: Lesson Plans

For each lesson, produce the following in order:

**1. Lesson Heading** — dark navy background (1F3864), white text, bold, 14pt.
Format: `Lesson [N] — [Phase Name] — [Learning Focus in brief]`
Add a small phase badge (coloured rectangle text) immediately below.

**2. LO Table** — 3 rows × 2 columns. Left column 4500 DXA, right column 10898 DXA.
Header row: dark navy fill (1F3864), white text.

```
We are learning to:          | [WALT — starts with a verb, specific]
We are learning this because | [1–2 sentences linking to enquiry or real purpose]
I will show this by:         | [Observable — visible in book or page]
```

**3. Overview paragraph** — 2–3 sentences summarising the lesson flow.

**4. Lesson Detail Table** — 3 columns:
- Phase (I Do / We Do / You Do) — 2000 DXA
- Activity (what happens) — 8698 DXA
- Teacher Notes (resources, AFL, differentiation) — 4700 DXA

I Do / We Do / You Do is NOT rigidly linear:
- May start with You Do (cold task / diagnostic)
- May cycle multiple times
- You Do may be collaborative
- Label clearly when varied: "You Do (first)", "We Do (class share)", etc.

**5. Resources Row** — single row spanning all columns, light grey fill (F5F5F5).
Format: `Resources: [list of teaching and pupil resources]`

## Notes

- Enquiry runs alongside the Being a Reader unit — planned separately
- Writing outcome typically sits in Phase 3, lesson 1–2 (draft) + lesson 3 (edit)
- Enquiry outcome typically sits in Phase 3, lesson 3–5 (create + share)
- In 1-week (7-lesson) units, outcomes are tightly interleaved — plan carefully
